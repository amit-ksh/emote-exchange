## Voiceover

Twitch emotes are a community's shared language. Emote Exchange turns that attention into a game the whole chat can play: a live emote market, using free play Coins with no cash value.

The streamer runs one local app. From this dashboard, they connect Twitch, manage the market, and add a browser source to OBS. The overlay shows Prices, top Traders, recent activity and market moments.

Here is the real Twitch connection in action. A viewer buys two shares by typing a command in chat. The app processes the Trade, saves the Holding, updates the Feed, and replies through the bot. The portfolio command reports the viewer's Coins and shares. Selling settles the Trade and updates the balance again. These are actual chat messages and app responses, recorded together.

Two different chatters using a recognized emote can list it. Prices follow its share of recent chat attention, with diminishing credit for repeated Uses from one person. The streamer can pause trading, moderate Traders and give Grants. Greetings reward returning participation with Streaks. Live analytics separates real chat from simulation.

For larger crowds, this clearly labeled SIM run exercises the same engine. The keyless demo works without Twitch credentials, and simulated activity never sends messages to Twitch.

Codex helped build the project from its specifications and acceptance checks. The market engine is pure: time comes in, and persistence, display and reply effects come out. A real review found that simulated buys happened before emotes were listed. The scenario was corrected to establish Listings first. Automated tests cover settlement, recovery and output isolation; Windows and Ubuntu CI both pass.

Emote Exchange makes chat attention visible, gives viewers a shared game, and keeps the streamer in control. One local process, a Twitch chat, and an OBS browser source.
